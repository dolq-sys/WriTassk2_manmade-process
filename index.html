02
The passive voice
Look at the clues again. What do the verbs have in common?

DiscoverNatural vs man-made

Last lesson · Natural process
The eggs hatch into tadpoles.
Today · Man-made process
The beans are roasted.
1
Who does the action in each sentence?
Natural: the eggs do it themselves. Man-made: people or machines do it.
2
Why don't we write "Workers roast the beans"?
The doer is not important. The focus is the material (the beans), so we use the passive.
3
Look at are roasted. What is the pattern?
be (is / are) + past participle (VpII)

The rulePassive voice in a process

Material / product+is / are+VpII
The pasteismixed with sugar. The beansareroasted. The doughis thenbaked in an oven.
Always present simple: is / are + VpII
is / are + then + VpII shows the order
by + machine only when useful: dug by a digger
Never by workers / by people

04
Model: recycling paper
First we analyse the diagram. Then we write.

The diagram below shows how recycled paper is made.Analyse the diagram first

1
How many main phases are there?
Four: making pulp, filtering, cleaning, making recycled paper.
2
What goes in? What comes out?
Used paper goes in. Rolls of recycled paper come out.
3
What is added to the paper?
Water and chemicals (phase 1); water and soap, plus air (phase 3).
4
What is removed?
Ink (cleaning) and water (under the heated rollers).
5
Linear or cyclical?
Linear: it goes in one direction, from used paper to a new product.

05
Linking the stages
Not "Then … Then … Then …", and not "after which" in every sentence.

DiscoverWhich paragraph reads better?

VERSION A
The pulp is transferred to a cleaning tank. Water and soap are added. Then air is pumped in. Then the ink is removed. Then the pulp is fed into a machine.
VERSION B
The pulp is transferred to a cleaning tank, where water and soap are added and air is pumped in so that the ink can be removed. Once the pulp has been cleaned, it is fed into a machine.
?
Why is version B better?
Fewer, longer sentences · no repeated "Then" · it shows how the stages are connected (place, purpose, time).

Your turn: making bricks

The diagram illustrates the process used to manufacture bricks for the building industry.Analyse the diagram first

1
How many stages are there?
Seven: digging, mixing, shaping, drying, firing, cooling, packaging and delivery.
2
First stage? Last stage?
Clay is dug from the ground → the bricks are packaged and delivered.
3
What is added?
Sand and water (stage 2).
4
Which numbers must you report?
Drying: 24–48 hours · Kiln: 200°C → 1,300°C · Cooling: 48–72 hours
5
Is there a choice at any stage?
Yes, stage 3: a mould OR a wire cutter.
